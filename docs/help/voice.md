what: how Ada, Navilife's assistant, sounds in Talk, the morning brief and the call assistant.
how: Pick a voice there and play a sample.
tip: On a slow connection the first words can take a moment; offline it says "Using the phone's voice while offline" once and carries on in the phone's own voice.
tip: Ada talks a touch quicker than before, with a small natural pause after a quick reaction ("Oh nice."), and each sentence of her answer is made ready as she writes it, so there are no gaps between them (Amazon voices; Google's voices keep their own pace).
tip: Ask → More → Activity shows how quickly the voice answered; clips heard over Bluetooth or car audio are marked "+ Bluetooth", about 0.2 s more.
tip: In Talk the first words of an answer come with the answer itself, so Navilife starts speaking without a second wait; Activity marks those "with the answer".
tip: At the top, Ada calls you lets you choose whether it calls you by your name or "sir" in Ask, Talk and Hello; the choice follows you to every device.
tip: Under it, What Ada remembers lists a line about each proper chat (kept 30 days in your own data) so Ask, Talk and Hello can pick up where you left off; choose Don't remember to start fresh each time (it forgets them too), or Forget all.
tip: At the foot of Ada's voice, Speed shows your last 20 hellos and spoken answers: how long each took to start (aim: a hello within 0.6 s of your tap, an answer within 1.5 s of you finishing) and where the wait went (sending, the answer, the voice). It is kept on this device only and holds times, never what was said; Clear empties it.
tip: Under Speed, On the server shows how long Navilife's server took over the last day's answers, quick look-ups and conversation apart: the middle time to the first words, the first sentence (aim: within 1 second), the voice ready and the whole answer. Times only, never what was said.
tip: Spoken answers, and every quick look-up, come from Ada's quicker model so her first sentence starts sooner; a typed open question (advice, planning, a long one) still gets the larger, more thoughtful one. Her long standing instructions and the conversation so far are reused from one turn to the next rather than read afresh, which is quicker and costs less.
tip: A tap on the orb speaks at once: the hello is made ahead in Ada's voice and kept ready on this device (for this part of the day and the next hour), and while the first sentence of an answer plays the rest is already on its way, so there is no pause between them.
tip: While Ada works out an answer she never says "One sec" or any other holding line: the orb shows Thinking at once, and only if an answer takes more than about two and a half seconds does one soft, breath-like tone play. Most answers start well before that, so there is just the orb and then her voice.
tip: The moment you tap the orb, open Talk or start speaking, Navilife's server gets its line to the AI and to Ada's voice ready, so the first answer doesn't wait for a fresh connection. Nothing you say is sent for this, and it costs nothing.
tip: On the phone, a quick question that only looks something up ("what's on tomorrow?", "when's the bin day?") goes off the moment Navilife hears you finish, while the phone is still writing down your last words; if those words turn out different, it asks again with them. Anything that adds, moves, sends or pays, chats and Money questions always wait for your final words.
tip: With headphones (AirPods, a Bluetooth headset, the car) you can answer the hello while it is still speaking: the microphone opens with it, ignores Navilife's own voice and stops the hello once you say a couple of words, so none of yours are lost. On the speaker, talking over the hello stops it a little quicker than other lines.
