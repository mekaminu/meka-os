what: your spending by category, month by month, from the statements of your bank and card accounts, kept behind your fingerprint (Touch ID on the Mac). Read-only: Navilife never moves money, pays anything or keeps a bank password, and nothing it shows is financial advice.
how: Download a statement from your bank's website or app as CSV (some call it a spreadsheet or Excel file). Open Ask → Life → Money, confirm it's you, type which account it is (e.g. Current account) and tap Import a statement file, then pick the file. Importing the same months again adds nothing twice.
mac_how: Open Ask → Life → Money, confirm with Touch ID, type which account it is and click Import a statement file…, then choose the CSV file you downloaded from your bank.
tip: Each month shows what you spent, what came in, and each category with its share of the month; for a finished month it says how that compares with your average over the 6 months before, and lights a category that's well over it. Use ‹ and › to go back a month or forward again.
tip: A payment in the wrong category? Tap it and pick the right one: every payment to that shop goes there from now on, on the Fold and the Mac alike, with Undo.
tip: A card statement that lists purchases as positive amounts is read the right way round (purchases as money out); the line after an import says so.
tip: Remove takes one account and its transactions off every device; Delete all money data at the foot clears everything. Your bank keeps its own records.
tip: Connecting your banks directly (read-only, through Open Banking) comes once it's set up; a statement file works for any bank or card meanwhile.
tip: This month also shows tips from your own numbers: a habit and what halving it would save ("Coffee 19× · £71 in the last 30 days"), a category already well over its 6-month average, and a regular payment that went up. Hide tips like this turns that kind off on both devices (Undo, or Show them again later).
tip: Regular payments lists what's taken about once a month for about the same amount (subscriptions, the gym, bills), with when it was last taken, when it's next due and the monthly and yearly total.
tip: Worth a look leads this month when a payment deserves a second look: the same amount at the same shop twice within a day, a payment much bigger than usual or a first big one somewhere new, and fees (foreign, late, interest).
tip: Not you? Your bank's app can stop or dispute it; Navilife never acts on your accounts. That's fine puts an alert away on both devices, with Undo.
tip: Forecast at your pace takes your average whole month (what comes in, what goes out, what's left over) and says where that leaves you in 6 and 12 months. It needs two whole months of statements; it's an average, not a promise, and not financial advice.
tip: What if: tap Halve on one of your biggest everyday categories, or type an extra amount a month (£200, £50 a week, £1,200 a year), and the 6- and 12-month lines say how much more it would leave you. What-ifs stay on the device you're using and go when Money locks.
tip: Refunds on the way lists each refund on Waiting for and checks your statements for it: when the shop's money is back, Close it takes it off Waiting for (Undo). Two weeks on with nothing back, it lights and drafts a chase; Copy the chase puts it on the clipboard for you to send in the shop's chat or email, and marks it chased. Navilife never sends it.
tip: Until payday finds your payday from your pay coming in (two months or more of the account it goes into) and says what you've spent since and what's still to go out before it: regular payments, and the bills, subscriptions and club fees with a cost on Lists → Renewals.
tip: With that account's balance it says what's safe to spend until payday, and a day, and lights when it's tight. A statement file with a Balance column gives it, or type it under Until payday (−£50 if overdrawn).
tip: Cards: tap It's a card next to a card's account, then type what its statement says: the statement balance, the minimum, the day of the month it's due, the limit and when a 0% period ends (Edit details, any time).
tip: Each card says when it's due (lit three days out), "pay in full to avoid interest" (a charge card is paid in full), how much of the limit the statement uses (lit from 75%) and any interest or late fee charged on it.
tip: Three days before a card payment, at 9 in the morning, a heads-up says it's due (no amounts on the lock screen). Tap Paid on the card once you've paid it and the reminder stops until next month (Undo).
tip: A 0% period gets a heads-up a month and a week before it ends. A card due before payday counts in what's still to go out. Navilife never pays anything.
