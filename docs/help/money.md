what: your spending by category, month by month, from the statements of your bank and card accounts, kept behind your fingerprint (Touch ID on the Mac). Read-only: Navilife never moves money, pays anything or keeps a bank password, and nothing it shows is financial advice.
how: Download a statement from your bank's website or app as CSV (some call it a spreadsheet or Excel file). Open Ask → Life → Money, confirm it's you, type which account it is (e.g. Current account) and tap Import a statement file, then pick the file. Importing the same months again adds nothing twice.
mac_how: Open Ask → Life → Money, confirm with Touch ID, type which account it is and click Import a statement file…, then choose the CSV file you downloaded from your bank.
tip: Each month shows what you spent, what came in, and each category with its share of the month; for a finished month it says how that compares with your average over the 6 months before, and lights a category that's well over it. Use ‹ and › to go back a month or forward again.
tip: A payment in the wrong category? Tap it and pick the right one: every payment to that shop goes there from now on, on the Fold and the Mac alike, with Undo.
tip: A card statement that lists purchases as positive amounts is read the right way round (purchases as money out); the line after an import says so.
tip: Remove takes one account and its transactions off every device; Delete all money data at the foot clears everything. Your bank keeps its own records.
tip: From your banks: your accounts and cards fill Money by themselves through Lunch Flow, read-only, every few hours. Refresh fetches them now (it takes a minute); the line says when they were last fetched and how many accounts.
tip: A bank asks you to agree again every 90 days or so: Money then says "Reconnect … in Lunch Flow" and Open Lunch Flow takes you there. Navilife never logs in to your bank and never moves money.
tip: A statement you imported before for the same account counts once: its lines that match the bank's are left out, and its balance isn't added twice. A bank account you Remove stays off until you tap Sync them again.
tip: This month also shows tips from your own numbers: a habit and what halving it would save ("Coffee 19× · £71 in the last 30 days"), a category already well over its 6-month average, and a regular payment that went up. Hide tips like this turns that kind off on both devices (Undo, or Show them again later).
tip: Regular payments lists what's taken about once a month for about the same amount (subscriptions, the gym, bills), with when it was last taken, when it's next due and the monthly and yearly total.
tip: Worth a look leads this month when a payment deserves a second look: the same amount at the same shop twice within a day, a payment much bigger than usual or a first big one somewhere new, and fees (foreign, late, interest).
tip: Not you? Your bank's app can stop or dispute it; Navilife never acts on your accounts. That's fine puts an alert away on both devices, with Undo.
tip: Forecast at your pace takes your average whole month (what comes in, what goes out, what's left over) and says where that leaves you in 6 and 12 months. It needs two whole months of statements; it's an average, not a promise, and not financial advice.
tip: What if: tap Halve on one of your biggest everyday categories, or type an extra amount a month (£200, £50 a week, £1,200 a year), and the 6- and 12-month lines say how much more it would leave you. What-ifs stay on the device you're using and go when Money locks.
tip: With a Hyperliquid address followed, What if also offers Trading as it's gone: your actual trading result a month (last month and the one before, after fees and funding, in pounds at today's rate; the last 30 days until the month is added up). Tap it and the 6- and 12-month lines add it on top of your statements, a loss as a loss.
tip: Refunds on the way lists each refund on Waiting for and checks your statements for it: when the shop's money is back, Close it takes it off Waiting for (Undo). Two weeks on with nothing back, it lights and drafts a chase; Copy the chase puts it on the clipboard for you to send in the shop's chat or email, and marks it chased. Navilife never sends it.
tip: Ask or Talk about your money: "how much did I spend on takeaways last month?", "what's coming out this week?", "when's payday?", "can I afford £300 on Saturday?". It's answered on the device from Money, never sent to the AI.
tip: While Money is locked, a money question shows Unlock Money to answer: your fingerprint (Touch ID on the Mac), then the answer; for five minutes after, money questions (Talk's follow-ups too) are answered straight away.
tip: Until payday finds your payday from your pay coming in (two months or more of the account it goes into) and says what you've spent since and what's still to go out before it: regular payments, and the bills, subscriptions and club fees with a cost on Lists → Renewals.
tip: With that account's balance it says what's safe to spend until payday, and a day, and lights when it's tight. A statement file with a Balance column gives it, or type it under Until payday (−£50 if overdrawn).
tip: Cards: tap It's a card next to a card's account, then type what its statement says: the statement balance, the minimum, the day of the month it's due, the limit and when a 0% period ends (Edit details, any time).
tip: Each card says when it's due (lit three days out), "pay in full to avoid interest" (a charge card is paid in full), how much of the limit the statement uses (lit from 75%) and any interest or late fee charged on it.
tip: Three days before a card payment, at 9 in the morning, a heads-up says it's due (no amounts on the lock screen). Tap Paid on the card once you've paid it and the reminder stops until next month (Undo).
tip: A 0% period gets a heads-up a month and a week before it ends. A card due before payday counts in what's still to go out. Navilife never pays anything.
tip: Net worth adds up each account's balance, what each card's statement says you owe, and things you type that aren't in these accounts: "Pension £42,000", "Savings elsewhere 12k", "Car £8,500", "Car loan £3,200" (a loan, mortgage, debt or a minus sign counts it as owed). Type the same name again to update it; Remove takes one off (Undo).
tip: An account with no balance isn't counted yet: type its balance in the field under it. The line under the total shows the last 12 months, each account's balance worked back through its statements (cards and what you typed stay at today's figures); tap a month for its total. Not financial advice.
tip: Trading: type your Hyperliquid public address (the 0x… one, never a key) in Money's TRADING field, with a name if you like ("Main 0x…"). Nothing here can trade; Remove stops following an address. Under it, YOUR TRADES adds up the last 30 days: trades closed and how many won, fees and funding, the best and worst trade and each coin's result after fees.
tip: Trading alerts: under ALERTS in Money's TRADING, pick how near liquidation a position may get before Navilife alerts you (10% unless you change it; this one comes even in quiet hours) and how far below this month's high an account may fall before a heads-up (20%). The alerts never show amounts; tap one to open Money.
tip: Two more trading alerts under ALERTS: A big order (a heads-up when one order is worth 5× the account unless you choose 2× or 10×, within two hours of it filling) and Funding paid in a day (a heads-up once a day when the last 24 hours' funding costs 1% of the account, or 0.5% or 2%). Both are measured against the account, so no amounts are shown.
tip: P&L · LAST 30 DAYS in Money's TRADING draws your trading profit and loss day by day over the last month, ending at Now, with the high and low; tap a day for its figure and the account value then. It's profit and loss only, so a deposit or withdrawal doesn't move the line.
tip: Your Hyperliquid accounts count in NET WORTH in pounds, at the European Central Bank's daily dollar rate (the row says the rate and its day), and the trading equity says "about £…" beside the dollars.
tip: Today's header says "Trading · up today · 2 positions open" (no amounts: the figures stay in Money), lit when a position is near liquidation or an account is at your drop level; the morning brief says the same in a sentence (the Short brief only when it's lit).
tip: EXPORT FOR TAX under Money's TRADING saves a spreadsheet (CSV) of a UK tax year (6 April to 5 April): every order with its size, average price, value, closed profit and loss and fee, each day's funding per coin, and each account's totals. Tap the year, wait while Navilife's server reads it from Hyperliquid (up to a minute), then choose where to save it. Facts only, not tax advice.
tip: The tax export gives US dollars and pounds: each row is also converted at HMRC's monthly exchange rate for its month, shown in its own column. A month whose rate couldn't be read is left blank and the file says so.
tip: From the 1st of each month Money's TRADING shows last month in review: trades closed and won, the result after fees and funding, against the month before, the best and worst coin, the biggest win and loss. A heads-up at 9:00 on the 1st says it's ready (no amounts). From Hyperliquid's public data, UK months, perpetuals only; facts, not advice.
tip: Navilife's server reads each address from Hyperliquid's public data every few minutes while a position is open, hourly otherwise: equity, profit and loss for today, the week, the month and all time (deposits and withdrawals aren't counted as profit), and each open position with how far it is from liquidation (lit within 10%).
tip: SHOW IN under Money's TRADING total picks Dollars or Pounds. In pounds every amount on TRADING (equity, profit and loss, open positions, the curve, your trades and the monthly review) is shown at the European Central Bank's daily dollar rate, with the dollars beside the equity; prices stay in dollars, as Hyperliquid quotes them. The choice follows you on both devices.
tip: SIDE HUSTLE AND TAX in Money adds up your side hustle for the UK tax year so far (from 6 April): what came in, what went out by category, and the profit. Switch on Side hustle account on the account its money goes through, or tap a payment and choose Business (every payment to or from them counts, on any account).
tip: Under SIDE HUSTLE AND TAX pick the share of the profit to put aside (10% to 40%); it's your choice, not a tax bill. The next Self Assessment dates are listed, lit within a month. Not tax advice: Navilife never files or pays anything.
tip: Under SIDE HUSTLE AND TAX, YEAR-END EXPORT saves a spreadsheet (CSV) of a tax year's side hustle money for your accountant: every payment in and out with its account and category, then money in, money out by category and the profit. It's made on your device from your own statements; tap last year or this year so far, then choose where to save it.
tip: SHARED WITH (your partner's name from About you) keeps a running who-paid-what. Tap a payment and choose Shared for just that one, or Always shared for every payment there (the weekly shop, the energy bill); type what they paid below ("Tesco £42.50"). It says who owes whom at the share you pick (half each unless you choose 40, 60 or 70%).
tip: Settled up starts the count again: only what's paid after it counts (Undo puts it back). Navilife never moves money or asks anyone to pay; income and transfers are never counted as shared.
tip: CHALLENGES in Money (this month) are goals you set yourself from your own statements: No-spend days counts the days with no everyday spending (bills, subscriptions and fees don't break one), with a dot per day, the streak and your best this month.
tip: Fun money gives you an allowance for the week, Monday to Sunday ("£50"), for eating out, coffee, takeaways, going out, shopping and clothes; a cap limits one category for the month ("Eating out £150", "Takeaways £60"). Each shows a bar, what's left (brass from 90%) and how many weeks or months in a row you stayed within it. Remove takes one off (Undo); they follow you on both devices.
tip: PAYING ABROAD in Money (this month) looks at the last year of your statements: payments in another currency (a line with EUR, USD, € or a Visa rate) and the foreign fees charged for them, per account. It says which card charged no foreign fees, which did and about what share, and the one to use abroad.
tip: When your phone or Mac is in another time zone, or a trip on Trips starts within two weeks, the card to use leads the top of Money. Ask "which card should I use abroad?" for the same answer, on your device. It's from your own statements, not a product suggestion or financial advice.
